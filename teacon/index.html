Every good prompt answers four questions

நல்ல கேள்விக்கு நான்கு பகுதிகள்

A lazy prompt gets a generic answer that could be for any child in any country. Answer these four, and the AI writes for your class.

WHOயார்?Who should the AI be? An experienced Class 7 science teacher.
WHATஎன்ன?What should it do? A 40-minute lesson plan on photosynthesis.
FOR WHOMயாருக்கு?For whom? 45 mixed-ability children, more at home in Tamil, no lab.
HOWஎப்படி?In what form? A table, one hands-on activity, three questions, an exit ticket.

For teachers

ஆசிரியர்களுக்கு

Lesson planபாடத் திட்டம்

WHO: You are an experienced [science] teacher in a Tamil Nadu school.
WHAT: Create a [40]-minute lesson plan on [photosynthesis] for Class [7].
FOR WHOM: [45] students of mixed ability; many are more comfortable in Tamil than English. Resources: [a blackboard, chart paper, leaves from the school garden; no lab].
HOW: Give it as a table with columns for minutes, what the teacher does, what students do, and materials. Include one hands-on activity, three check-for-understanding questions (one in Tamil), and a 5-minute exit ticket.

Worksheet at three levelsமூன்று நிலை பயிற்சித்தாள்

WHO: You are a [maths] teacher who makes learning fun.
WHAT: Make a worksheet on [comparing and adding fractions] for Class [5].
FOR WHOM: One class with three groups: children who are still struggling, children working at grade level, and fast learners who need a challenge.
HOW: Three sections labelled Support, Core and Challenge, with five questions each. Use everyday Tamil Nadu contexts. Keep the language simple and add an answer key at the end.

Question paper with answer keyவினாத்தாள்

WHO: You are an experienced examiner for Class [9] [science].
WHAT: Write [10] multiple-choice questions on [the topic].
FOR WHOM: Students preparing for the [mid-term] exam, with a mix of easy, medium and hard questions.
HOW: Mark each question's difficulty. Give an answer key with a one-line reason for each answer. No trick questions.

Explain it simply, in Tamilஎளிய தமிழில் விளக்கம்

WHO: You are a patient teacher who explains with stories.
WHAT: Explain [the concept] in simple Tamil.
FOR WHOM: A Class [6] student who found the textbook explanation hard.
HOW: Under 150 words, with one example from daily life in Tamil Nadu. Then ask three short questions to check understanding.

Report-card commentமதிப்பீட்டு அட்டைக் குறிப்பு

WHO: You are a caring class teacher.
WHAT: Write a report-card comment for a Class [4] student. Do not use a name.
FOR WHOM: The child's parents.
HOW: About 40 words, warm and specific. Strengths: [reads fluently, helps classmates]. Next step: [neater working in maths]. No labels such as "weak" or "slow".

AI drafts, the teacher decides. Add what only you know about the child before it goes home.

Rubricமதிப்பீட்டு அளவுகோல்

WHO: You are an experienced [English] teacher.
WHAT: Create a rubric for [a 2-minute oral presentation].
FOR WHOM: Class [8] students, who will see the rubric before they start.
HOW: A table with 4 criteria and 4 levels (Beginning, Developing, Proficient, Excellent), with short, child-friendly descriptions.

AI-proof an assignmentபணியை மறுவடிவமைத்தல்

WHO: You are an assessment designer for schools.
WHAT: Redesign this assignment so students must show their own thinking: [paste your assignment].
FOR WHOM: Class [8] students who all have AI at home.
HOW: Give three versions: one AI-free (done in class), one AI-assisted (students show their drafts and the prompts they used), and one AI-integrated (students critique an AI answer). One short paragraph each.

For principals and managements

தலைமையாசிரியர்கள் மற்றும் நிர்வாகத்திற்கு

Parent circular in Tamil and Englishபெற்றோர் சுற்றறிக்கை

WHO: You are the principal of a Tamil Nadu school, writing to parents.
WHAT: Write a short circular about [the Parent-Teacher Meeting on Saturday, date, time, place].
FOR WHOM: Parents of Classes [6 to 10]. Many read Tamil more comfortably than English; some are first-generation school parents.
HOW: Warm and respectful, under 120 words. Tamil version first, then English. [Mention what parents should bring.]

Ask a Tamil teacher to read the Tamil version before it goes out.

Marks analysisமதிப்பெண் பகுப்பாய்வு

WHO: You are an experienced academic coordinator.
WHAT: Analyse the attached marks for Class [8] [maths]. Students are coded; there are no names.
FOR WHOM: The class teacher and the principal, who have 10 minutes to read this.
HOW: Give (1) the weakest topics, with the class average for each; (2) the students who need support, with one line on why; (3) any link between attendance and marks; (4) a simple two-week remedial plan. One short table, plain language.

Before you upload: replace names with roll numbers or codes, and remove phone numbers and addresses.

First draft of a school AI policyபள்ளி AI கொள்கை

WHO: You are an education policy adviser for Indian schools.
WHAT: Draft a one-page AI-use policy for our school, Classes [6 to 12], for our teachers to discuss and change.
FOR WHOM: Teachers, students and parents.
HOW: Plain language, in English and Tamil. Cover what students and teachers may use AI for, what is not allowed, the student-data rule (no names, photos or marks in public AI tools), and how every assignment states whether it is AI-free, AI-assisted or AI-integrated.

Write the final version with your teachers, not for them.

Summarise a long circular or orderசுருக்கம்

WHO: You are a principal's assistant.
WHAT: Summarise this document: [paste the text].
FOR WHOM: Busy teachers who need to know what changes for them.
HOW: Five bullet points in English and the same five in Tamil. Then list any deadlines and who must act on each.

Reply to an upset parentபெற்றோருக்குப் பதில்

WHO: You are a calm, respectful school principal.
WHAT: Draft a reply to a parent who is upset that [their child's marks have dropped]. Do not include names.
FOR WHOM: A worried parent who wants to be heard.
HOW: Under 150 words. Acknowledge the concern, explain the facts simply, and offer a time to meet. Nothing defensive.

FAQ for parents: "Should my child use AI?"பெற்றோருக்கான கேள்வி–பதில்

WHO: You are the principal of a Tamil Nadu school, writing to parents.
WHAT: Write a one-page FAQ for parents titled "Should my child use AI?". Answer five questions: Should my child use AI for homework? From what age? Is my child's data safe? How will the school know the work is my child's own? How can I help at home?
FOR WHOM: Parents of Classes [6 to 10]. Many read Tamil more comfortably than English; some are first-generation school parents.
HOW: Warm, simple and reassuring, under 200 words in each language. Give the Tamil version first, then the English. Explain the school's traffic light: red (no AI), amber (AI for ideas, and the child shows her work), green (AI used and then checked). End with one line inviting parents to speak to the class teacher.

Check the facts before it goes home: each AI tool sets its own minimum age (ChatGPT, for example, requires users to be at least 13, with a parent's permission under 18). Ask a Tamil teacher to read the Tamil version.

To use with students

மாணவர்களுடன்

Teach children to make AI ask the questions, and to check its answers before they trust them.

A tutor, not an answer machineவிடை அல்ல, வழிகாட்டி

WHO: You are a patient tutor.
WHAT: Help me solve this problem without giving me the answer: [the problem].
FOR WHOM: I am a Class [9] student.
HOW: Ask me one question at a time and wait for my reply. Give a hint only if I am stuck twice.

Check an AI answerAI பதிலைச் சரிபார்த்தல்

WHO: You are a fact-checking coach for students.
WHAT: Here is an answer I got from AI: [paste the answer].
FOR WHOM: A Class [10] student learning to verify information.
HOW: List each claim in it, tell me how I could check each one (which book, website or person), and mark the claims most likely to be wrong.

Three checks before you drink

நம்புவதற்கு முன் மூன்று கேள்விகள்

AI predicts the next word; it doesn't know. It is fluent, not always right. Teach every child these three questions.

  1. Where did this come from?

    எங்கிருந்து வந்தது?

  2. Who else says so?

    வேறு யார் சொல்கிறார்கள்?

  3. Does it make sense to me?

    இது எனக்குச் சரியாகப் படுகிறதா?

AI drafts. The teacher decides. Relationships stay human.

A traffic light for every assignment

ஒவ்வொரு பணிக்கும் ஒரு போக்குவரத்து விளக்கு

Detectors can't reliably tell AI writing from a child's, so design the task instead. Tell students the level when you set the work.

Red: AI-free · AI இல்லை

Done in class, on paper or out loud. What can this child do on her own? Vivas, classroom writing, solving problems in front of the teacher.

Amber: AI-assisted · AI உதவியுடன்

AI allowed for ideas or feedback. The child shows the process: drafts, the prompts she used, what she changed and why.

Green: AI-integrated · AI ஒருங்கிணைப்புடன்

The task requires AI and a critique of it. "Ask AI, then find three things it got wrong."

The student-data rule

மாணவர் தகவல் விதி

Never put anything that identifies a child into a public AI tool: no names, photos, addresses or phone numbers. Use codes that can't be traced back, and keep the list of codes offline.

குழந்தையை அடையாளம் காட்டும் எந்த விவரத்தையும் பொது AI கருவிகளில் ஒருபோதும் உள்ளிடாதீர்கள். பெயருக்குப் பதில் குறியீடுகளைப் பயன்படுத்துங்கள்.

India's DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, set strict conditions on children's personal data, including verifiable parental consent, with narrow exemptions. Most duties apply from about mid-2027. Build the habit now.
