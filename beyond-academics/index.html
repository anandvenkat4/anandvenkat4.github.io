Your 7-day challenge

  1. Day 1: set up or clean up your GitHub and LinkedIn.

  3. By day 7: write a one-paragraph plan for your first real project, and tell two friends.

1Problem solving

“Companies don’t pay you to know answers. They pay you to figure them out.”

  • NPTELFree IIT and IISc courses: DSA, DBMS, OS, networks, and your own core branch.
  • CS50x from HarvardThe best free introduction to computer science and problem solving.
  • LeetCodeCoding interview practice. Start with Easy problems, one a day.
  • HackerRankPractice tracks in the style of many campus coding tests.
  • IndiaBIXAptitude and reasoning practice for placement round one.

After you solve a problem, explain your approach out loud. If you can’t explain it, you got lucky.

3AI fluency with judgment

“If AI does your thinking, you’ll graduate with a degree and no skill. If AI coaches your thinking, you’ll learn in one year what took me three.”

  • Elements of AIA free, non-technical introduction to AI. Good for every branch.
  • Kaggle LearnShort, hands-on courses: Python, pandas, machine learning.
  • Google Machine Learning Crash CourseMachine learning fundamentals with exercises.
  • Practical Deep Learning (fast.ai)Build working deep learning models from lesson one.
  • Hugging Face LearnFree courses on LLMs and AI agents.
  • DeepLearning.AI short coursesOne-to-two-hour courses on building with AI; many are free.
  • Google ColabFree notebooks in the browser, with a GPU. No laptop upgrade needed.

Never submit code you can’t explain line by line.

4Communication: speak, write, listen

“Your idea is only as good as your ability to explain it.”

  • BBC Learning EnglishFree lessons for everyday and workplace English.
  • Google Technical WritingFree courses on writing clearly as an engineer.
  • TED TalksWatch how good speakers structure a story, then try it with your project.

Record yourself explaining your best project in two minutes on your phone. Watch it back. Repeat until a non-engineer would follow it. Your accent doesn’t matter; clarity does.

5Teamwork

“Nobody ships software alone.”

  • First ContributionsMake your first open-source pull request, step by step.
  • Good First IssueBeginner-friendly issues in real open-source projects.
  • Google Summer of CodePaid open-source work with a mentor over the summer.
  • Smart India HackathonThe national student hackathon. Check for the current edition.
  • DevfolioHackathons across India, many open to student teams.

Form a squad of three or four friends. Meet for one hour every week to build something together.

6Ownership and professionalism

“‘That’s not my module’ is a career-limiting sentence.”

  • How to ask a good questionStack Overflow’s guide. The same rules work with your seniors at work.
Habits to practise in college
  • Take charge of something real: a club role, an event, a lab setup.
  • The 30-minute rule: try alone for 30 minutes, then ask, saying what you tried.
  • Meet every deadline you set. If you’ll miss one, say so early.
  • Never say “almost done” when you haven’t started.

7Learning agility and resilience

“The most important skill isn’t Python or prompting. It’s learning how to learn.”

  • SWAYAMGovernment of India’s free online courses, many with credit transfer.
  • roadmap.shStep-by-step learning roadmaps for developer roles.
  • Microsoft LearnFree learning paths for cloud, AI and data.
  • AWS Skill BuilderHundreds of free cloud courses.

Please don’t pay ₹50,000 for a certificate that a free course teaches better. Treat every rejection as data: write down what they asked and what you’ll fix.

AI prompts to copy

Use AI as a tutor, not a ghostwriter.

Tutor

Explain [topic] using a Tamil movie example. Then give me three practice problems. Don’t give me the answers.

Mock interviewer

Act as an interviewer for a fresher software role. Ask me one question at a time and give me feedback after each answer.

Code reviewer

Review this code like a senior engineer. List the bugs and explain each one. Don’t rewrite it for me.

Pitch coach

Here is my project in five lines. Ask me questions first, then help me turn it into a two-minute explanation a non-engineer can follow.
